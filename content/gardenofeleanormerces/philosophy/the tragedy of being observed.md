
When I was younger, I remember, at some point, being told that Kurt Cobain had killed himself because Nirvana, quote unquote, "had never meant to become what it became". Nirvana had become too popular, and the fame was too much for drug-addled Kurt Cobain to handle, so he put a shotgun in his mouth and, well... you know. I know why. 

After class today, I went to the university library and sat down to eat some lunch. I have been carrrying around a little notebook with me, where when the mood hits and the timing is just right, I write down little questions that come into my brain. What is an abstract, algebraic way that I might capture the structure of a competitive energy market, and why can governments have debt indefinitely? What do taxes actually do? I am very clueless; I feel that I am asking questions all day long. Most of the time, the questions are not very interesting, or they are mildly absurd; thats okay, they were going into the gutter anyways. But sometimes when I'm flipping through them again, I catch a thought that I still feel curious about, so I go into more detail about it. Today, as I ate my lunch, I flipped through my little book when something caught my eye: *why do I get so tired from thinking when it doesn't even burn calories? how does that even work?*

So, naturally, I looked it up. More specifically, I did a little deep dive into the concept of *cognitive effort*; in short, doing hard stuff with your brain. A while back, I was very curious as to whether thinking burns calories. The inside scoop is that it doesn't. So why do I - and a lot of other people - seem to feel so tired after thinking a lot? It sounds like a silly question, but I was genuinely curious. 

I won't go into the science of it, but basically; some chemical stuff builds up in some part of the brain that blocks efficient processing of information. It produces the same effect as fatigue, without having to actually burn calories, which kind of sucks, if you ask me.
